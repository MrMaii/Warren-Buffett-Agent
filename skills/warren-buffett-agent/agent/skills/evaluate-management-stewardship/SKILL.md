# Evaluate Management Stewardship

## Operating stance

Judge observable decisions over presentation skill. Trust can reduce bureaucracy only after integrity, ability, incentives, and bad-news behavior meet a high threshold.

## Inputs

Collect multiple years of:

- Shareholder letters, filings, transcripts, and guidance changes.
- Reinvestment, acquisitions, divestitures, buybacks, dividends, debt, and share issuance.
- Compensation metrics, ownership, related-party transactions, and governance.
- Operating outcomes relative to original claims and industry conditions.
- Restatements, control issues, regulatory actions, turnover, and succession evidence.

Use primary records and quote context, not reputation summaries.

## Method

### 1. Separate three dimensions

- `Integrity`: truthfulness, fairness, bad-news speed, and treatment of owners/stakeholders.
- `Ability`: operational judgment, talent selection, adaptation, and understanding of economics.
- `Allocation`: conversion of retained capital into per-share value.

A high score in one does not cancel failure in another.

### 2. Build a promise-to-outcome ledger

For each material commitment record:

```text
Date | Claim/target | Capital committed | Outcome | Explanation
What management learned | Whether behavior changed
```

Distinguish reasonable forecast error from changed definitions or concealed failure.

### 3. Audit capital behavior

Evaluate purchase price, financing, dilution, timing, integration, divestiture discipline, and return of excess capital. Compare per-share outcomes with plausible alternatives.

### 4. Inspect incentives

Ask:

- What behavior does compensation reward?
- Can management benefit while owners lose per share?
- Are targets easily adjusted or acquired?
- Is ownership meaningful and purchased with personal capital?
- Who bears downside and tail risk?

### 5. Test candor

Look for prompt discussion of mistakes, bad segments, changed assumptions, and corrective action. Praise by name and criticism by category can improve tone, but must not erase accountability.[S07]

### 6. Test decentralization quality

Where autonomy is claimed, verify:

- Decision rights are clear.
- Operators have information and accountability.
- Capital, integrity, and major risk are escalated.
- Headquarters does not replace ownership with process theater.[S06]

### 7. Evaluate talent and succession

Assess whether management recruits strong people, grants them useful autonomy, develops successors, and avoids dependence on one hero.

### 8. Search for disconfirming conduct

Prioritize repeated adjusted metrics, promotional language, costly empire building, delayed write-downs, hidden dilution, related-party advantages, and blame shifting.

### 9. Reach a stewardship verdict

- `High trust`: strong evidence across integrity, ability, and allocation.
- `Conditional trust`: capable but requires named controls or capital limits.
- `Low trust`: incentives/candor/allocation undermine owner alignment.
- `Disqualifying`: integrity or governance failure makes economics uninvestable.
- `Insufficient evidence`: history or disclosure is too limited.

## Output contract

Produce a `Stewardship Dossier`:

1. Integrity evidence and counterevidence.
2. Operating ability.
3. Capital-allocation scorecard.
4. Incentive alignment.
5. Candor and bad-news behavior.
6. Delegation/succession quality.
7. Verdict, confidence, and required controls.
8. Events that would upgrade or downgrade trust.

## Stop and escalate

Stop definitive claims when identity, legal, control, or accounting facts are disputed. Escalate allegations of fraud, fiduciary breach, discrimination, or legal violations to qualified investigators/counsel; do not diagnose intent from weak signals.

## Failure modes

- Equating confidence or charisma with ability.
- Treating founder ownership as automatic alignment.
- Ignoring purchase price in acquisition evaluation.
- Excusing repeated errors because management is candid.
- Using stock price as the sole management score.
- Calling low bureaucracy good without accountability.
- Turning cultural differences into moral judgments.

## Safety and truthfulness

Use neutral, evidence-based language. Distinguish misconduct allegations, confirmed findings, poor economics, and ordinary error. Avoid claims about private motives.

## Self-review

- Did I score integrity, ability, and allocation separately?
- Is every major conclusion tied to observable behavior?
- Did I compare promises with outcomes?
- Do incentives transfer downside to others?
- Is trust paired with escalation and accountability?
- What evidence would change the verdict?
