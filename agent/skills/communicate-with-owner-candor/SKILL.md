# Communicate with Owner Candor

## Operating stance

Write as if the reader entrusted savings or scarce resources to you and roles could be reversed. Candor requires facts, responsibility, and correction—not merely a humble tone.

## Inputs

Collect:

- Audience, decision rights, horizon, and what they already know.
- Reported results and the economic yardsticks that matter.
- Good developments, bad developments, errors, and unresolved uncertainty.
- Original promises/assumptions and current evidence.
- Capital decisions, alternatives considered, and next actions.
- Sources, dates, and disclosure/legal constraints.

## Method

### 1. Build a truth ledger before drafting

Separate:

```text
Verified fact | Economic interpretation | Estimate | Unknown
Original expectation | Actual outcome | Responsibility | Correction
```

Do not let tone work begin until this ledger is complete.

### 2. Lead with what owners need to know

Open with the decision or economic result, the most material bad news, and the relevant boundary. Do not begin with slogans, mission statements, or market applause.

### 3. Explain the owner economics

Use a small number of meaningful measures and bridge accounting to economic reality. State why each yardstick matters and what it omits.[S01, S08, S09]

### 4. Report good and bad symmetrically

Apply the same materiality and causal standard. Do not present bad news only after pages of success. Separate controllable errors from external conditions without using the latter to escape responsibility.

### 5. Admit errors precisely

Use:

```text
I/we expected:
What actually happened:
What we got wrong:
Economic impact:
What changed now:
How recurrence will be prevented:
```

Avoid passive phrases such as “mistakes were made.”

### 6. Use plain mechanisms and restrained analogy

Translate jargon once. Use no more than one analogy per major idea, and remove it if it cannot coexist with direct evidence. Humor may lower defensiveness but must not minimize harm.

### 7. Treat people fairly

Specific praise can be named. Criticism should focus first on system, category, and correction, while retaining accurate accountability when responsibility or safety requires it.[S07]

### 8. Disclose capital allocation and alternatives

Explain where resources went, what was rejected, why price/terms mattered, and how per-share/owner value is expected to change. State what would make the decision look wrong.

### 9. Close with action and review

Give commitments, owners, timing, evidence still needed, and the event/date for the next update. No generic optimism ending.

## Output contract

Produce one of these with the same truth standard:

- `Owner Decision Memo`: judgment, economics, downside, alternatives, action, review.
- `Shareholder Letter`: owner relationship, yardsticks, good/bad developments, errors, capital allocation, outlook boundaries.
- `Board Update`: decision, evidence, controls, accountable owner, escalation.

Always include sources/periods for material facts and a section named `What could change this conclusion`.

## Voice controls

- Calm, plain, evidence-led, lightly conversational.
- First-person responsibility where authorized.
- Long causal sentence followed by short action sentence.
- No imitation of accent, age, private anecdote, recurring catchphrase, or “Oracle” mythology.
- Do not quote Buffett unless the user asks; prefer source-linked paraphrase.

## Stop and escalate

Stop publication when material facts are unverified, disclosure obligations are unclear, responsibility is disputed, or legal/HR/safety language requires review. Provide a fact ledger and requested decisions to the reviewer.

## Failure modes

- Promotional copy wearing a humble tone.
- Burying bad news.
- Admitting error without economic impact or correction.
- Using market price as the only performance measure.
- Naming individuals to deflect system responsibility.
- Long quotations or Buffett cosplay.
- Generic disclaimers instead of concrete uncertainty.

## Safety and truthfulness

Protect confidential data and follow disclosure rules. Never invent personal memories, quotes, financial figures, current holdings, authorization, or organizational responsibility.

## Self-review

- Did owners learn the material bad news early?
- Are facts, estimates, and unknowns visibly separate?
- Is responsibility active and specific?
- Does every metric connect to owner economics?
- Are alternatives and rejected actions disclosed?
- Can a reader see what changes the conclusion and when the next update occurs?
- Would the document remain credible with all stylistic flourishes removed?
