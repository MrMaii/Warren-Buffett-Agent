# Construct Concentrated Portfolio

## Operating stance

Concentration follows demonstrated understanding and asymmetric value; it is never a substitute for them. Survival, liquidity, correlation, and unknown risk cap every position regardless of conviction.

## Inputs

Require for each candidate:

- Circle-of-competence gate.
- Business-quality and moat verdict.
- Conservative value range and margin of safety.
- Downside/leverage map.
- Liquidity, ownership terms, and thesis-break conditions.

Also require portfolio mandate, horizon, liabilities, cash needs, legal limits, and existing look-through exposures. If these are personal circumstances, provide education and scenario analysis only; do not prescribe individual trades.

## Method

### 1. Define the mandate and survival floor

State maximum acceptable permanent loss, required liquidity, concentration/legal limits, and obligations that must be met without selling risky assets.

### 2. Create a conviction ledger

For every candidate record:

- Understanding confidence.
- Business durability.
- Conservative value spread.
- Evidence quality.
- Permanent-loss severity.
- Thesis falsifiers.

Do not compress these into one unexplained score.

### 3. Look through labels

Map actual economic drivers: customer, geography, commodity, funding source, regulation, technology, currency, and counterparty. Different tickers can be the same bet.

### 4. Bound loss before sizing upside

Estimate position-level loss under a severe plausible scenario, including dilution and illiquidity. Define size by the portfolio's ability to survive being wrong, not by desired return.

### 5. Account for correlation under stress

Use scenario-based correlation, not only historic price covariance. Test simultaneous funding closure, customer weakness, regulatory change, and common factor exposure.

### 6. Value cash and optionality

Cash is not a residual mistake. Charge its opportunity cost while crediting liabilities, resilience, and future deployment capacity.

### 7. Rank, cap, and stage

Group candidates:

- Core: strongest evidence and durable economics.
- Developing: thesis plausible but evidence/margin incomplete.
- Watch: price or evidence not ready.
- Reject: failed integrity, survival, or competence gate.

Stage only when new evidence can improve the decision.

### 8. Set rebalance triggers

Revisit when value/price converges, thesis evidence changes, risk correlations rise, liabilities change, or a superior opportunity appears. Avoid activity for its own sake.

## Output contract

Produce a `Portfolio Architecture Memo`:

1. Mandate and survival floor.
2. Look-through exposure map.
3. Candidate conviction ledger.
4. Severe-loss and liquidity table.
5. Correlated stress scenarios.
6. Illustrative allocation ranges or concentration caps, not personalized orders.
7. Cash/optionality role.
8. Thesis-break and rebalance triggers.

## Stop and escalate

Stop allocation ranges if mandate, liabilities, liquidity, ownership restrictions, or look-through exposures are unknown. Escalate tax, fiduciary, regulatory, derivatives, and personal suitability questions.

## Failure modes

- Concentrating because a name is familiar.
- Equal-weighting as automatic diversification.
- Using historic price correlation as the only overlap measure.
- Sizing from upside while ignoring permanent loss.
- Treating cash as failure.
- Letting one macro/funding factor dominate hidden exposures.
- Turning a research portfolio into personal trade instructions.

## Safety and truthfulness

Use current position and market data with timestamps when available. Do not execute trades, guarantee diversification, or tailor orders to an individual's finances. Make illustrative assumptions explicit.

## Self-review

- Did every concentrated candidate pass understanding, quality, value, and survival gates?
- What common factor links supposedly different positions?
- Can liabilities be met without forced sales?
- Is size bounded by being wrong?
- Are cash and alternatives valued explicitly?
- Are rebalance triggers thesis-based rather than price-noise-based?
